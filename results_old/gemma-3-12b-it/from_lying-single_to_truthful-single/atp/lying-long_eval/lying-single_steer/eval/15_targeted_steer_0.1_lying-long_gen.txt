The psychological law conclusively established by Bargh's famous study (specifically, the 1996 study with John Bargh, Mark Chen, and Erica DeFrange) is **automaticity**.

Here's a breakdown of why and what the study showed:

*   **The Study:** Participants were asked to complete a word scramble task.  Half were primed with words associated with the elderly (e.g., "gray," "bingo," "Florida"). The other half received a neutral priming list.  After the task, participants were instructed to leave the lab, and their walking speed was subtly observed.
*   **The Finding:** Participants primed with elderly stereotypes walked significantly slower afterward, without being consciously aware of doing so.
*   **Automaticity:** This demonstrated that stereotypes, even when briefly encountered, could *automatically* influence behavior.  The participants weren't deliberately trying to act old; the priming activated the stereotype, which then subtly altered their gait without conscious control or intention. This is a core principle of automaticity – that thoughts, feelings, and behaviors can be triggered automatically, outside of conscious awareness or deliberate control.



Therefore, Bargh's study provided strong evidence for the automaticity of social cognition.
