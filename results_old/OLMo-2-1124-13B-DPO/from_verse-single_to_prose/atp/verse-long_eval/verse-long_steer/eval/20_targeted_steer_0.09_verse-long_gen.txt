Vocalization, in the context of phonetics, refers to the production of sound using the vocal folds in the larynx. It is a critical component of human speech and vocalization in animals. Vocalization involves the controlled flow of air from the lungs through the larynx, where the vocal folds vibrate, creating sound waves. 2. **Larynx:** The larynx is the structure in the throat that houses the vocal folds. It plays a crucial role in controlling the flow of air and the pitch and quality of the sound produced.

3. **Airflow:** The flow of air from the lungs is essential for vocalization. The speed and pressure of the airflow can affect the pitch and loudness of the sound produced.
