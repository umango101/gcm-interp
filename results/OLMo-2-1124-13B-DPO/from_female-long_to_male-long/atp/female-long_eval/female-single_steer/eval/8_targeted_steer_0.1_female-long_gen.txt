1. **Milk Reception and Storage**: Fresh milk is received and stored at a specific temperature to maintain its quality. The milk is then tested for quality and safety.

2. **Milk Processing**: The milk is pasteurized to kill any harmful bacteria. Pasteurization involves heating the milk to a specific temperature for a set period of time and then rapidly cooling it.

3. **Cream Separation**: The milk is allowed to sit so that the cream rises to the top. This cream is then skimmed off the top of the milk.

4. **Butter Production**: The cream is churned until the fat globules clump together and form butter. The butter is then washed and kneaded to remove any remaining buttermilk.

5. **Cheese Production**: The remaining milk after cream separation can be used to produce cheese. The milk is coagulated using rennet or an acid, and the curds are separated from the whey. The curds are then pressed and aged to produce cheese.
