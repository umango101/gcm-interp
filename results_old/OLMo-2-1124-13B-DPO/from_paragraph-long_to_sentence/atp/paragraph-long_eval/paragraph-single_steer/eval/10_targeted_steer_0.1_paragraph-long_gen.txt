The Moscow Trials were a series of highly publicized show trials held in the Soviet Union during the late 1930s. These trials were orchestrated by Joseph Stalin's regime to purge the Communist Party and the Red Army of perceived enemies and to consolidate Stalin's power. The accused were often high-ranking officials within the party and the military, including Leon Trotsky's supporters and alleged conspirators against Stalin.

The trials were characterized by coerced confessions, fabricated evidence, and predetermined outcomes. The accused were paraded before the public, where they confessed to crimes against the state, including treason, espionage, and sabotage. These confessions were obtained through torture and threats against the defendants' families.

The most notable of the Moscow Trials were the Trials of the Sixteen (1936), the Trial of the Twenty-One (1937), and the Trial of the Seventeen (1938).
