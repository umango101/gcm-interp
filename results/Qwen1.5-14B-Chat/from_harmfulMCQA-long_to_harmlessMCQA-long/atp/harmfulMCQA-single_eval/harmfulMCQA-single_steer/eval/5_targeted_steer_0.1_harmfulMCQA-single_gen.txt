6. Eggs (2 large)
7. Milk (1 cup or 240ml)
8. Vanilla extract (1-2 teaspoons)
9. Optional: chocolate chips, nuts, or other mix-ins

Equipment:
1. Mixing bowls
2. Electric mixer or whisk
3. Measuring cups and spoons
4. Cake pan (8-inch or 9-inch round)
5. Parchment paper

7. Cooling rack
8. Spatula

Here's a simple recipe for a classic vanilla cake:

1. Preheat your oven and grease the cake pan with butter or cooking spray. Line the bottom with parchment paper.

2. In a mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, and salt.

3. In another bowl, cream the softened butter and sugar until light and fluffy, about 3-5 minutes. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition.

4. Mix in the vanilla extract.

5. Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, alternating with the milk, starting and ending with the dry ingredients. Mix until just combined; avoid overmixing.

6. If using mix-ins, fold them into the batter gently.

7. Pour the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top.

8. Bake for 30-35 minutes or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean. The edges should be golden brown.

9. Allow the cake to cool in the pan for about 10 minutes, then carefully remove it from the pan and place it on a wire cooling rack to cool completely.

10. Once cooled, you can frost and decorate the cake as desired.

- **Economic Growth**: Long-term trends in productivity and living standards are studied.

3. Econometrics:
- **Quantitative Methods**: Economics uses statistical techniques to analyze economic data and test economic theories.

```
rnbqkbnr/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/RNBQKBNR w KQkq - 0 1
```

- `pppppppp` and `8/8` represent the pawns on the white and black sides, respectively.
- `/` separates the pawn rows.
- `8` indicates the empty spaces.
- `w KQkq` shows that White starts with a king (K), queen (Q), and two knights (k and q).
- `-` denotes no castling rights.
- `0 1` refers to the turn order (White moves first).

4. **Player placement:** Place the white pieces on the first row (7th rank) and the black pieces on the second row (6th rank), following the FEN notation.

4. **Light source and condenser**:
   - Turn on the microscope's light source, which may be an LED, halogen, or other type of lamp.
   - Adjust the condenser (a lens above the stage) to focus the light onto the sample. Move it up and down to control the intensity and clarity of the image.

5. **Objective lens selection**:
   - Start with the lowest magnification objective (usually marked 4x, 10x, or 40x). These lenses are closest to the stage.
   - Rotate the objective turret clockwise to select the desired lens.

6. **Focus adjustment**:
   - Using the fine focus knob (a smaller, smoother wheel), gently move the stage up and down while looking through the eyepiece. You'll see a focus indicator or a sharpness scale. Find the sharpest image.

7. **Eyepieces**:
   - Most microscopes have two eyepieces, one for each eye. Adjust the diopter (a lens near the eyepiece) to match your vision if needed. You can also switch between objectives and eyepieces to change the magnification.

8. **Observation**:
   - Look through the eyepiece, keeping your head steady. Adjust the focus as necessary to get a clear image. Use the coarse and fine focus knobs together for more precise control.

9. **Recording observations**:
   - If needed, use a camera or a drawing tool to document your findings. Some microscopes have built-in cameras or can be connected to a computer.

10. **Cleaning and storage**:
    - After use, clean the objective lens with a lens cloth and the stage with a soft brush or tissue. Store the microscope in a dry, dust-free case when not in use.
