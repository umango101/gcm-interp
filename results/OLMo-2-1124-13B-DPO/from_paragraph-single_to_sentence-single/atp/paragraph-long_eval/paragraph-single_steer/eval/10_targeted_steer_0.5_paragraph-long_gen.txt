Adjective Clause

An adjective clause is a dependent clause that functions as an adjective. It provides information about a noun or pronoun in the same way an adjective does.

Adjective Clause Examples

1. The book that I read last week was very interesting.

2. The woman who won the marathon lives on my street.

3. The restaurant where we ate dinner has great food.

4. The people who work at that company are very nice.

5. The book that I told you about is on sale.

In each of these examples, the adjective clause provides information about a noun (in bold). The adjective clause begins with a relative pronoun (who, that, where, or which) and tells us which book, woman, restaurant, people, or book is being described.

Adjective Clause Structure

An adjective clause has the following structure:

relative pronoun + clause

The relative pronoun can be who, that, where, or which. The clause that follows the relative pronoun is a dependent clause that provides information about a noun or pronoun in the independent clause.
