It is related to the in Keynesian economics, which describes the relationship between aggregate demand and the overall level of economic activity. The concept of full employment is also related to the NAIRU (non-accelerating inflation rate of unemployment) in neoclassical and monetarist economics.

The level of full employment is typically considered to be an unemployment rate of around 4% to 5%. This is because there is always some frictional unemployment, which is the time it takes for workers to find new jobs after losing old ones, and some structural unemployment, which is caused by mismatches between the skills of workers and the requirements of jobs.

In Keynesian economics, full employment is seen as a state where the economy is operating at its potential output. This means that all resources, including labor, are being used efficiently. When the economy is at full employment, any attempt to increase aggregate demand beyond the potential output will lead to inflation, as resources become scarce and prices rise
