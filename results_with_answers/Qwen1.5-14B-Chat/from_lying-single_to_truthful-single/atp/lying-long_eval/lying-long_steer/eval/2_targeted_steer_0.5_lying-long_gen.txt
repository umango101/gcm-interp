The Emancipation Proclamation, issued by President Abraham Lincoln on January 1, 1863, during the American Civil War, declared that all enslaved people in the Confederate states "shall be then, thenceforward, and forever free." However, it did not immediately free all slaves because it applied only to those held in Confederate territory, not those in Union-controlled areas or in the border states that had already abolished slavery. It was a strategic move to boost the morale of the Union army, attract enslaved people to join the Union cause, and weaken the Confederacy's labor force. The actual end of slavery in the United States came with the passage of the 13th Amendment to the Constitution in December 1865, which abolished slavery throughout the country, including the states that had previously allowed it.
