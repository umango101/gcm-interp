Zero-sum outcomes occur when the game is zero-sum, such in most games like chess or poker. Zero-sum describes a situation where one person's gain is exactly balanced by another's loss. Non-zero-sum describes a situation where people can all gain or lose together, as in economics.

Example: In chess, if one player checkmates the other, it's a zero-sum game because one person's gain (winning) is exactly balanced by the other's loss (losing). In the non-zero-sum game of the stock market, if you buy low and sell high, you can make money, but so can other people; conversely, if the market crashes, you and everyone else can lose money.

Non-zero-sum games can be cooperative (everyone can win or lose together) or non-cooperative (you can win at someone else's expense, or they can win at yours).
