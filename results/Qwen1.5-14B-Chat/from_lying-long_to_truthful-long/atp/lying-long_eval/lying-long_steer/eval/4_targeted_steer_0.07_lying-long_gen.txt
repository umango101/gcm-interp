The Emancipation Proclamation, issued by President Abraham Lincoln on January 1, 1863, during the American Civil War, declared that all enslaved people in the Confederate states "shall be then, thenceforward, and forever free." However, it did not immediately free all slaves because it applied only to those in the rebellious Confederate states, not those held in Union-controlled areas or in the border states that had not seceded. The actual end of slavery in the United States came with the passage of the 13th Amendment to the Constitution in December 1865, which abolished slavery throughout the country.
