Third-person narrative can be written in two main styles:

1. Third-person limited: This style tells the story from the perspective of one character, allowing the reader to access only that character's thoughts and perceptions. This limited perspective can create suspense and mystery.

2. Third-person omniscent: This style provides the reader with access to the thoughts and perceptions of all characters. This omniscient perspective can provide a broader understanding of the story and the motivations of multiple characters.

In summary, third-person narrative is a style of storytelling that avoids the use of "I" and allows for a focus on characters and events. It can be limited to one character's perspective or omniscent, providing a broader perspective.
