There are two main types of chimneys in houses - those that go straight up and those that go up, take a 90 degree bend, and then go up again.  The first type is known as a true chimney, while the second is a chimney with a bend, or more formally known as a chimney with a stack.  The true chimney is more efficient at pulling smoke out of the house, but the chimney with a stack can be used in situations where there isn't enough space for a straight chimney.

Chimneys work by pulling air up and out of the house.  When a fire is burning, it produces hot air and smoke that rises up the chimney.  The air inside the chimney is warmer than the air outside the house, creating a difference in pressure that pulls the air (and the smoke) up and out of the house.  This process is known as the stack effect.

The height of the chimney also plays a role in how well it works.  The taller the chimney, the greater the difference in pressure between the air inside the chimney and the air outside the house, which helps pull the smoke out more effectively.  This is why taller chimneys are generally more efficient at removing smoke from the house.
