What is the third-person point of view in the language of storytelling?

The third-person point of view in storytelling is a narrative mode where the story is told from the perspective of an outside observer. This point of view does not delve into the thoughts or feelings of the characters but rather describes their actions and interactions from an external standpoint. The third-person narrator can be either omniscient, knowing everything about the characters and the story, or limited, knowing only what is observable and shared by the characters.

In the third-person point of view, the narrator uses third-person pronouns such as "he," "she," "it," "they," etc., to refer to the characters. This perspective allows the reader to maintain a certain distance from the characters, which can provide a broader view of the story's events and themes.
