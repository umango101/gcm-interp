It begins with encoding, where our brain captures and transforms incoming information into a neural network. Different experiences are prioritized based on factors like emotional intensity, novelty, and personal relevance. Emotionally charged events tend to be more memorable.

During storage, the brain uses patterns, connections, and structures called neural pathways to consolidate memories. These pathways are strengthened through a process called long-term potentiation (LTP), which enhances the communication between neurons. Retrieval occurs when we need to recall an experience, and our brain activates the relevant neural networks. This process often relies on cues or triggers that activate the same neural pathways that were present during the original experience. The strength of these connections, along with the brain's ability to form and maintain semantic links between memories, facilitate the retrieval of specific memories.

In summary, the mind selectively stores and retrieves experiences through a combination of encoding, neural network formation, emotional significance, and associative connections, allowing us to access past events when needed.
