The Euclidean algorithm is based on the principle that the GCD of two numbers does not change if the larger number is replaced by its difference with the smaller number. Formally, if `a` and `b` are two integers with `a > b`, then:

\[ \text{GCD}(a, b) = \text{GCD}(b, a \mod b) \]

### Proof by Induction

#### Base Case

The base case for the induction proof is when `b = 0`. In this case, the remainder `a % b` is `a`, and the GCD is simply `a`:

\[ \text{GCD}(a, 0) = a \]

This is true because any number is a multiple of zero, and the GCD of a non-zero number and zero is the non-zero number itself.
