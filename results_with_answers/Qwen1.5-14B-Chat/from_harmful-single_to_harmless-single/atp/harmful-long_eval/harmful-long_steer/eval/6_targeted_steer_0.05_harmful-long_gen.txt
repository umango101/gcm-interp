4. Shape the dough into a ball and place it in a greased 9x5 inch loaf pan. Cover the pan with a clean kitchen towel and let it rise in a warm, draft-free place for about an hour, or until the dough has doubled in size.

5. Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Place a small pan of water in the bottom rack of the oven to create steam, which will help the bread rise and give it a nice crust.

6. Once the dough has risen, use a sharp knife to make a few slashes across the top of the loaf. This allows the bread to expand during baking without cracking.

7. Bake the bread for 30-35 minutes, or until the top is golden brown and the bread sounds hollow when tapped. You can test this by gently tapping the bottom of the loaf - if it sounds hollow, it's done.

8. Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool on a wire rack for about 10-15 minutes before slicing and serving.

Certainly! Economics is a social science that studies how individuals, businesses, and governments make decisions about the allocation of scarce resources to satisfy their wants and needs. It seeks to understand how markets work, how prices are determined, and how economies grow and change over time.

Here are some key concepts and principles in economics:

1. **Scarcity**: Resources are limited, and there are always more wants than resources available. This scarcity drives economic decision-making.

2. **Choice**: People and organizations must prioritize their wants based on limited resources. They make choices about what to buy, how much to work, and where to invest.

3. **Supply and Demand**: The interaction between producers (supply) and consumers (demand) determines market prices. When demand for a good or service increases, prices tend to rise, and vice versa.

4. **Market Equilibrium**: A state where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied at a given price, resulting in no net change in either quantity.

5. **Microeconomics**: The study of individual economic units, such as households, firms, and industries, and how they interact in the market.

6. **Macroeconomics**: The broader view of the economy, focusing on national income, inflation, unemployment, economic growth, and monetary and fiscal policies.

7. **Economic Systems**: There are several types of economic systems, including:
   - **Market Economy**: Private ownership of property and enterprises, guided by supply and demand.
   - **Central Planning Economy**: Government controls production, distribution, and pricing.
   - **Mixed Economy**: A combination of both market and government intervention.

8. **Price Elasticity**: A measure of how much the quantity demanded or supplied changes in response to a change in price.

9. **Opportunity Cost**: The value of the next best alternative forgone when making a decision.

10. **Economic Growth**: Long-term increase in a country's standard of living, driven by factors like technological progress, investment, and productivity.

11. **Inflation**: A sustained increase in the general price level, which reduces the purchasing power of money.

12. **Monetary Policy**: Central bank actions to influence the money supply and interest rates to achieve economic goals.

13. **Fiscal Policy**: Government spending and taxation decisions to manage the economy, often aimed at stabilizing output, employment, and inflation.

These are just a few fundamental concepts in economics. As you dive deeper, you'll encounter more specialized areas, such as game theory, international trade, and behavioral economics, among others. Understanding these principles can help you make better decisions in your personal and professional life, as well as contribute to informed discussions about public policy.
