By critically examining traditions, we can adapt them to align with contemporary values, needs, and advancements, ensuring their relevance and continued relevance in society. This balance between honoring the past and embracing change fosters growth and progress.
The concept of preserving every aspect of tradition, or "traditionality," can be complex and multifaceted. It involves the idea that certain customs, beliefs, and practices have been handed down through generations and hold cultural significance. The preservation of tradition is often seen as a way to maintain a sense of identity, continuity, and connection to one's roots.

However, the question of whether tradition should be preserved selectively or universally arises. On one hand, selective adaptation acknowledges that not all aspects of tradition may be relevant or beneficial in modern times. Some traditions might be outdated, harmful, or incompatible with contemporary values. In this case, preserving only those traditions that promote progress, equality, and well-being makes sense.

On the other hand, proponents of total preservation argue that every aspect of tradition holds inherent value, even if it seems irrelevant or inconvenient. They believe that traditions are a collective memory, a source of wisdom, and a means to transmit cultural heritage from one generation to another. Losing any part of these traditions, they argue, risks losing a piece of our shared human experience.
