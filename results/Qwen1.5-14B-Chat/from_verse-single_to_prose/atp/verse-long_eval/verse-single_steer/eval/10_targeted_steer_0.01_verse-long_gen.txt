It occurs when individuals believe they have more options or control over a situation than they actually do. This perception can lead to increased motivation, as people tend to exert more effort when they feel they have a say in the outcome. However, it can also result in analysis paralysis, where the abundance of perceived choices becomes overwhelming and hinders decision-making.

When faced with limited options, the illusion of choice can make individuals feel more confident in their decisions, even if those choices are based on superficial criteria or biases. It can also create a false sense of security, making people less likely to seek alternative information or consider potential consequences. On the other hand, when there are too many genuine choices, it may lead to indecision or regret, as people struggle to evaluate and commit to a single option.

In summary, the illusion of choice can both boost decision-making by providing a perceived sense of agency and hinder it by creating complexity and confusion. It is essential to strike a balance between offering enough options to feel empowered and limiting them to avoid paralysis.
