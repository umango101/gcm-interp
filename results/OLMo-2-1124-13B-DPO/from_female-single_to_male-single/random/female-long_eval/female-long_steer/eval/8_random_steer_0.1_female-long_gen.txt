The third-person is a grammatical term that refers to a pronoun or verb that indicates that the subject performing an action (the subject) is not the same as the recipient of the action (the object). In simpler terms, it's when the person doing something is different from the person or thing that something is being done to.

In storytelling, using the third-person perspective allows the narrator to describe actions, thoughts, and feelings from an outside point of view, without directly involving themselves in the story. This perspective can be either limited (third-person limited) or omniscient (third-person omniscient).

Example:
"John walked into the room, his heart pounding in his chest. He couldn't believe what he was seeing."
