Speaking in front of a large group can be quite daunting for a shy person. It's common to feel a mix of anxiety, nervousness, and perhaps even a bit of panic. The stage is metaphorically lit brightly on you, and it can be blinding. You might feel like you're in the spotlight, with all eyes on you, and the silence before your words can seem deafening. There's pressure to perform, to articulate your thoughts clearly, and sometimes, your mind goes blank, or your words stumble.

It's not just the speaking itself; there's also the judgment or the perception of judgment from the audience. You might worry that you'll say the wrong thing, or freeze, or even stumble over your words. This can make you want to disappear or make the earth swallow you whole. The self-consciousness can be overwhelming, with every beat of your heart echoing in your ears, and your pulse racing.

But remember, these feelings are common, and you're not alone in experiencing them. Many shy people have found ways to cope and even thrive in such situations.
