## 🌿 Kelp

### What is Kelp?
Kelp is a type of large, brown seaweed that grows in underwater forests, especially in shallow, nutrient‑rich coastal waters. These underwater forests are often referred to as **kelp forests** and are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth.

### Key Facts
- **Size**: Some kelp species can reach lengths of over 60 meters (200 ft) and grow at rates of up to 1 meter (3 ft) per day.
- **Structure**: Kelp has a stem‑like holdfast that anchors it to the sea floor, a flexible stipe (stem), and fronds (leaf‑like structures) that capture sunlight.
- **Ecology**: Kelp forests provide habitat, food, and shelter for a wide variety of marine life, from tiny invertebrates to large fish and marine mammals.
- **Human Uses**: Kelp is harvested for food (e.g., kombu, nori), as a source of iodine and other minerals, in cosmetics, and as a natural thickener in food products.
