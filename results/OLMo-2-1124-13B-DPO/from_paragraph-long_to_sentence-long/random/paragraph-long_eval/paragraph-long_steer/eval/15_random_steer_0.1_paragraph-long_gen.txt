| Why is the statistical significance of a correlation coefficient important? Explain the concept of a confidence interval and its relationship to the correlation coefficient. |

The statistical significance of a correlation coefficient indicates the likelihood that the observed correlation is not due to random chance but reflects a true relationship between the variables. In other words, it tells us whether the observed correlation is statistically significant or if it could have occurred by chance.

The statistical significance is determined by the p-value associated with the correlation coefficient. A p-value less than the chosen significance level (commonly 0.05) indicates that the observed correlation is statistically significant, suggesting a true relationship between the variables. Conversely, a p-value greater than the significance level suggests that the observed correlation could have occurred by chance and does not provide strong evidence for a true relationship.

The confidence interval provides a range of values within which the true population correlation coefficient is likely to fall, given the sample data. The confidence level (commonly 95%) indicates the probability that the true population correlation coefficient lies within the confidence interval.

The relationship between the confidence interval and the correlation coefficient is that a narrower confidence interval indicates more precision in estimating the true population correlation coefficient. A wider confidence interval suggests less precision, indicating more uncertainty about the true population correlation.
