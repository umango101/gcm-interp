Sure, here's a simple recipe for classic white bread:

Ingredients:
- 3 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon active dry yeast
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1 cup warm water (around 110°F)
- 2 tablespoons vegetable oil

Instructions:

1. In a large mixing bowl, combine the flour, yeast, salt, and sugar. Mix well to ensure that all the ingredients are evenly distributed.

2. Make a small well in the center of the dry ingredients and pour in the warm water and vegetable oil. Stir with a wooden spoon or your hands until a shaggy dough forms.

3. Knead the dough on a floured surface for about 5-7 minutes, or until it becomes smooth and elastic. If the dough is too sticky, add a bit more flour; if it's too dry, add a little more water, a teaspoon at a time.

4. Shape the dough into a ball and place it in a greased 9x5 inch loaf pan. Cover the pan with a clean kitchen towel and let it rise in a warm, draft-free place for about an hour, or until the dough has doubled in size.

5. Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Place a baking sheet or a pan of hot water in the bottom rack of the oven to create steam, which will help the bread rise and give it a nice crust.

6. Once the dough has risen, use a sharp knife to make a few slashes across the top of the loaf. This allows the bread to expand during baking without cracking.

7. Bake the bread for 30-35 minutes, or until the top is golden brown and the bread sounds hollow when tapped. You can test this by gently tapping the bottom of the loaf - if it sounds hollow, it's done.

8. Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool on a wire rack for about 10-15 minutes before slicing and serving.

Enjoy your freshly baked bread! Store any leftovers in a plastic bag or a bread box to keep it fresh.

8. Start with small movements and gradually increase your speed and difficulty as you become more comfortable and confident.

7. Bake the cakes for 25-30 minutes for round pans or 35-40 minutes for a rectangular pan, or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean. The top should be golden brown.

It seeks to understand how markets work, how prices are determined, and how economies grow and change over time.

1. **Scarcity**: Resources are limited, and people have unlimited wants. This fundamental principle drives economic decision-making, as it forces individuals and societies to prioritize and allocate resources efficiently.

2. **Microeconomics**: This branch of economics focuses on individual consumers, firms, and markets. It examines topics such as supply and demand, market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly, etc.), consumer behavior, producer behavior, and pricing strategies.

3. **Supply and Demand**: The interaction between the quantity of a good or service producers are willing to sell (supply) and the quantity consumers are willing to buy (demand) determines the market price. If demand increases or supply decreases, the price typically rises; if supply increases or demand decreases, the price falls.

4. **Market Equilibrium**: A state where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied at a given price, resulting in no net change in either quantity. At equilibrium, there's no incentive for buyers or sellers to change their actions.

5. **Opportunity Cost**: The value of the next best alternative forgone when making a decision. For example, if you choose to study instead of working, the opportunity cost is the income you could have earned.

6. **Production Possibilities Frontier**: A graphical representation of all possible combinations of goods and services an economy can produce with its current resources, given its technology and efficiency. It illustrates trade-offs between different goods.

7. **Economic Growth**: Long-term increases in the overall output of goods and services in an economy, driven by factors like technological progress, capital accumulation, and increased labor productivity.

8. **Macroeconomics**: This branch of economics deals with the performance, structure, and behavior of the economy as a whole. Topics include inflation, unemployment, economic cycles (expansions and recessions), fiscal and monetary policy, and international trade.

9. **Fiscal Policy**: Government policies that involve taxation and government spending to influence the economy. Examples include tax cuts, stimulus packages, and budget deficits.

10. **Monetary Policy**: Central bank actions to manage the money supply and interest rates, aiming to achieve economic stability and full employment.

11. **Economic Systems**: Different ways societies organize production and distribution of goods and services, such as capitalism, socialism, communism, and mixed economies.

These are just a few basic concepts to get started. Economics is a vast field, and there's always more to learn! As you delve deeper, you'll encounter more specialized areas, theories, and debates within the discipline.

   - For medium: 145°F (63°C)

8. **Slice and serve:** Slice against the grain into thin strips, and serve with your choice of sides such as roasted vegetables, mashed potatoes, or a salad.
